### **2. Theory Summary**

#### **Overview of Computer Forensics**

- **Definition:** The practice of identifying, preserving, analysing, and presenting digital evidence in a legally admissible manner.
- **Purpose:** To investigate and solve digital crimes, ensuring justice through proper handling of evidence.

#### **Difference Between Computer Crime and Unauthorised Activities**

- **Computer Crime:** Illegal activities involving computers, such as hacking, identity theft, and malware distribution.
- **Unauthorised Activities:** Actions performed without permission, which may or may not be illegal (e.g., unauthorised access).

#### **Cyber Laws**

- Provide a legal framework for addressing computer-related crimes.
- Examples include the Information Technology Act in India, which governs electronic transactions, digital signatures, and cybercrime.

#### **Process of Computer Forensics**

1. **Identification:** Detecting potential sources of digital evidence.
2. **Preservation:** Securing evidence to prevent tampering or loss.
3. **Analysis:** Extracting and examining data.
4. **Documentation:** Recording all findings.
5. **Interpretation:** Understanding the evidence in context.
6. **Presentation:** Delivering findings in a court-admissible format.

#### **Need for Forensics Investigator**

- Ensures proper evidence handling.
- Helps solve crimes and support legal proceedings.
- Prevents evidence tampering and strengthens case integrity.

### **2. Theory Summary**

#### **Encoding, Encryption, and Hashing**

- **Encoding:**
    - Converts data into a specific format for efficient transmission or storage.
    - Not designed for security.
- **Encryption:**
    - Secures data by converting it into ciphertext using a key.
    - Reversible with the correct key.
- **Hashing:**
    - Generates a fixed-size hash value from input data.
    - Used for data integrity verification.

#### **Hash Collisions**

- Occur when two different inputs produce the same hash value.
- MD5 and SHA-1 are vulnerable to collisions.
- SHA-256 and SHA-3 are more secure alternatives.

#### **Hex Editor**

- **Purpose:** Allows forensic investigators to analyse binary files at the byte level.
- **Uses:**
    - Examine file headers.
    - Detect embedded data or hidden malware.

#### **Bit Rot**

- **Definition:** Gradual degradation or corruption of digital data.
- **Impact:** Loss of data integrity over time.
- **Prevention:** Regularly verify hashes of critical data.
